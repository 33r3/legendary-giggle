# Week One — Manual Playtest

Paper only. No code. The point is to find out whether the rates feel right and whether you actually walk.

---

## Currency: Fragments

- **Floor:** 4,000 steps/day. Steps below the floor earn nothing.
- **Rate:** 1 Fragment per 500 steps above the floor.
- **Daily cap:** 10 Fragments. (Keeps one huge day from breaking the economy.)

Fixed floor, not a trailing average. Desk laps count. Read the number off Health once at night.

---

## The Wager

Declare your set point **before the week starts**. Locked for the week.

| Set point | Sessions | Payoff bonus |
|---|---|---|
| Modest | 2 | +0 |
| Standard | 3 | +5 |
| Ambitious | 5 | +15 |

A **session** = outdoor walk started on the watch, 15 minutes minimum.

- Every session: roll d100 on your region's table. One roll per 15 min, max 3 per session.
- Hit your set point: one **Payoff roll** at week's end, adding the bonus to the d100.
- Miss it: no Payoff roll. Session rolls still count. No penalty beyond the forgone roll.

The bonus does two things. It bends the odds on every band, and it can push the roll past 100 into **Beyond** — a result reachable only on a Payoff roll with a bonus attached. Ambitious hits it on 86+, Standard on 96+, Modest never. Everything else on the table stays live on any roll, including Signature at a flat 1%.

## Log

One line per session. Keep it in Notes, a text file, whatever.

```
DATE | REGION | MINUTES | ROLLS | RESULTS | FELT?
```

Daily, one line:

```
DATE | STEPS | FRAGMENTS EARNED | RUNNING TOTAL
```

---

## The Three Questions

Answer these Sunday night. They're the actual output of the week — not the loot.

1. **Did you walk?** How many sessions against your declared set point. This is the only number that matters.
2. **Did any roll land?** Note which results produced an actual reaction versus which felt like nothing. If commons were tedious by day three, the floor is wrong. If nothing above Uncommon ever appeared, the bands are too tight for a one-week feedback loop.
3. **Did the wager have teeth?** Did the locked set point pull you out the door on a day you'd otherwise have skipped, or did you forget you'd declared it?

If the answer to #1 is no, that's the finding, and it's cheaper to learn now than after you've built the schema.
